# Landlord: unreturned earnest payment

## Editorial context

Redacted copies of substantive email bodies from the withdrawal and refund threads, ordered chronologically. Names, addresses, email addresses, property identity, and exact dates and times are replaced with bracketed labels. Repeated quoted history, logos, awards, and signature contact blocks are omitted; line wrapping is normalized. The $100/$500 distinction is retained because it explains the claim. An incomplete address-only message is omitted.

The correspondence documents an acknowledged refund that was repeatedly not delivered, rather than an explicit refusal to refund. The final retrieved reply promises a replacement check; actual receipt is not established here. References to the property's policy are the customer's assertions in the correspondence, not an independently reviewed lease or policy.

## What to learn from this exchange

The strongest message distinguishes the non-refundable fee from the refundable payment, identifies the withdrawal deadline and how it was met, and reconstructs prior requests. It reports an escalation already taken instead of threatening one. The reply supplies a written acknowledgment that the check had not been mailed and a concrete commitment to reissue it. A next follow-up could request the mailing date and tracking or payment reference, then verify receipt before closing the record. The earlier suggestion that a check was lost is explicitly a belief; later correspondence does not establish that it was ever sent.
